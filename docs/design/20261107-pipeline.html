Prompt to music video

One line of text in, a finished music video out, with Amber appearing and singing on camera. The plan blends the two AI workflows that have each worked on their own: the Amber + creative-director panel workflow, and the more conventional Offline workflow (one prompt per lyric line, event morphs, a strong black-and-white look).

HypotheticalEvery step below has been run on its own. No piece has yet gone from a prompt to a finished video with Amber in one pass. Offline-local went from lyrics to video with a generated song; the Amber pieces (Chronophobia, Slippery Road, Noble Hunt, Cuckoo) all started from a real song.

13

Retime, splice and finish

Sync to the beat by deleting or duplicating frames: each transition's halfway frame is found, and frames around it are dropped or repeated until it falls on its downbeat. No frames are interpolated, so a big correction can show as a small stutter or jump. The singing clips are cut in on shot boundaries. Grade (the Offline black-and-white look, or the universe's), deflare, light bursts on the drum runs, song muxed in. No beat pulse for R.Amber.

Can I run this on my machine?

The video model (stage 7) and the image models (stages 4 and 6) decide it. Each needs 33–68 GB of weights in GPU memory at once, so in practice a 128 GB unified-memory machine. Everything else fits a 24 GB graphics card. Figures are measured on the machines below unless marked; the full table is in docs/hardware-performance.md.

MachineWhole pipeline?What to expect
DGX Spark, 128 GBYesEverything at full precision. The reference speed: about 2 h of video rendering for a 4-minute song, plus stills. Two Sparks can split the stills and video between them.
Strix Halo (Ryzen AI Max+ 395), 128 GBYes, slowerAll models fit. MOSS stills ~1.7× a Spark, video ~2×, lip-sync 5.8 min per second of video. Two settings matter a lot: CPU offload off and AOTriton attention on (5× faster stills). The song and LLM stages weren't tried there.
MacBook Pro M5 Max, 128 GBMostlyVideo ~1.25× a Spark; Qwen-Image-Edit stills 33 s. MOSS-Image is blocked by one line in its own code (float64 on Apple GPUs); lip-sync is blocked by CUDA-only libraries. Those two steps need another machine.
RTX 4090 (24 GB)NoRuns stages 1, 2 (without the planner), 3, 5 and 8, and lip-sync at int8. Can't hold the image or video models without CPU offload, which this project doesn't use; offload would make them fit but slower (not measured).
64 GB unified memoryNot testedUnlikely without smaller (quantised) weights: LTX's weights alone are 68 GB, and Qwen-Image-Edit peaked at 67 GB.
Graphics card under 24 GBNot testedStages 3 and 8 are small; the LLM would need a smaller model.

Could this run in ComfyUI?

Technically yes: any Python step can be wrapped as a ComfyUI custom node, so the whole workflow could run inside it. The useful question is how much of it ComfyUI does well.

#StageFitWhy
1Create the songPoorThe three-songwriter workshop is a multi-turn LLM conversation with retries. LLM nodes can call llama-server, but the logic ends up as one big custom node.
2Record the songGoodACE-Step has native ComfyUI support and Whisper nodes exist; picking the best take is a small custom node.
3Disassemble the songMixedDemucs and librosa beat nodes exist in community packs; the MMS forced aligner and the drum-run detector would be custom nodes.
4Create the singer / actorGoodQwen-Image-Edit is native; MOSS-Image probably needs a custom node; proposing the universes is an LLM step (see 1).
5Design the video scenesPoorSame as 1: the directors' workshop is code, not a graph.
6Create key framesGood to draw, weak to checkDrawing fits naturally. “Flag it, then redraw up to 3 times” is a loop with a condition, which ComfyUI handles badly.
7Create the scenesGoodLightricks publishes official LTX nodes, LoRAs load natively, and community Wan wrappers run InfiniteTalk. Chaining each clip from the previous one's last frame across 20+ shots is the awkward part.
8Assemble the videoGoodVideo Helper Suite does the ffmpeg work, and frame-interpolation packs include FILM and RIFE. Beat-sync retiming would be custom.

Where it breaks down

  • Loops and decisions. The workflow runs once per shot, redraws stills that fail a check, and pauses twice for the artist's review. ComfyUI graphs run straight through, so in practice a script queues one graph per shot through ComfyUI's API, and that script is the pipeline.
  • One machine per graph. Spreading stills and video over several GPUs means a ComfyUI server on each box plus a dispatcher.
  • Memory. By default ComfyUI moves models to CPU memory when the GPU fills. Run it GPU-only, one job per box, and unload the 22B LTX model before loading the 14B Wan model.

Recommendation

Keep a script as the orchestrator for the LLM workshops, the audio analysis and the control flow, and use ComfyUI graphs for the per-shot image and video steps (stages 2, 4, 6, 7 and 8, where most of the GPU time goes). That gives a visual graph, hand fixes to single shots and shareable workflows, without rebuilding the workshops as nodes.

Not yet checked: whether current ComfyUI nodes support the exact versions used here (ACE-Step 1.5 xl-sft, LTX-2.5, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, InfiniteTalk int8, MOSS-Image).

Known weak points

  • Never run end to end from a prompt with Amber.
  • Amber holds in some media (charcoal, photographic) and not others (glass, oil, war photograph): facebench, 4 of 10 cases.
  • LTX drifts a drawn medium toward photographs unless the clip is named a hand-drawn animation.
  • Directors' workshops have overused explosions and spectacle; the Offline per-line prompts drew far fewer.
  • InfiniteTalk needs at least 3.2 s of audio and mouths some vowels wrongly ("ooo" for "I").
